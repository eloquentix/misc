02 — Summary

A dead battery wearing a dead board's costume.

The laptop went fully unresponsive overnight and presented exactly like a failed logic board — no power, no MagSafe LED. Two independent professionals confirmed it. Both were wrong.

The machine was tethered to an iPhone Personal Hotspot as its only network, then closed on battery and bagged. macOS cannot offload Wi-Fi scans on a Personal Hotspot, so when the phone drifted away the Wi-Fi subsystem woke the SoC to hunt for it — roughly once every 90 seconds for ~14 hours — flattening a full battery. At zero it crossed the Li-ion protection floor; sustained charging at home lifted it back over the line and it booted, healthy.

05 — Root cause

The phone left. The Mac hunted itself to death.

A Personal Hotspot is the one network type the Wi-Fi chip can't background-scan on its own. Tethered as the sole network and clamshelled on battery, the Mac had to wake its main processor for every scan — and once the phone wandered off, it scanned relentlessly to find it again.

Why it looked like a dead board. At the floor, the battery controller refuses to charge until a slow pre-charge raises cell voltage past a safety threshold — and during pre-charge no MagSafe LED lights and nothing responds. The fuel gauge also diverged from true voltage, reporting low single digits while the cell was already below the line. On a 60-second bench test, that is indistinguishable from failed hardware.

06 — The diagnosis failure

Two experts. One blind spot.

The Genius Bar and the recovery shop reached the same verdict — and that agreement felt like confirmation. It wasn't. Both used the same method: plug in briefly, see no light, declare the board dead. Shared method, shared blind spot. Two people running one flawed test is not two opinions — it's one, twice.

Agreement is only corroboration when the methods are independent. The correct test — 30 minutes on a known-good charger before condemning anything — was skipped twice.
07 — Lessons learned
Never clamshell a tethered laptop on battery.
If a phone hotspot is the only network and the phone leaves, the Mac scans itself flat. Kill Wi-Fi/Bluetooth — or Airplane Mode — before it goes in the bag.
“A cable is attached” ≠ “it is charging.”
The chain started on a dock that never charged. Confirm the menu bar reads Charging, not merely Power Adapter.
Deep-discharge mimics a dead board.
No LED, no response — for several minutes. Always sustain charge before believing any hardware-failure call.
Independent answers can share one error.
Two experts using the same quick test are not corroboration. Vary the method before you trust the consensus.

What went well

  • The instinct to just leave it charging at home beat two paid diagnoses.
  • Clock integrity held — the forensic reconstruction is trustworthy.
  • No data lost. No components replaced. The board was never faulty.

What went wrong

  • Tethered-on-battery-clamshelled is a known battery killer — done unknowingly.
  • Power config (keepalive / Power Nap on battery) was wake-permissive.
  • Professional diagnosis skipped the basic sustained-charge step.
09 — Update · Nov 7, 2026

It went dark again. Another Mac woke it.

After several more deep-discharge episodes, the same MacBook Air M4 stopped starting altogether. It spent the night with Apple chargers on every port and nothing happened. This time the fix wasn't a charger. It was cabled with USB-C to a second, running Mac (a MacBook Air M2 on its own battery) as the first step of a DFU firmware revive, and it came to life the moment the cable went in. No key combo, no Apple Configurator, no firmware revive, no data lost.

Both machines logged it: the host from the other end of the cable, the M4 in its own power log once it was back.

Why it worked is half answered. The host offered the M4 two things its chargers didn't. The first was a steady 5 V supply (up to ~15 W). That one is ruled out. In under two minutes, 15 W puts about 0.4 Wh into a pack that already held 13% (around 6 Wh), and the M4's log shows that charge was there the moment it woke. Power wasn't missing. The wake was. That leaves two candidates. One is the fresh USB-C attach: the M4's recorded wake reason is a port event, and a charger left plugged in all night sends no new event. The other is the Apple-to-Apple control channel, the one Apple Configurator uses to drive a Mac into DFU, which answered in 2.3 seconds. Next time, unplug a charger and plug it back into the same port first. If that wakes it, the attach event did the work.

When a Mac won't wake, the best charger in the room may be another Mac.

What keeps sending it to the floor. The hotspot storm explained how fast it drained in June, but it wasn't the whole story. The battery's own coulomb counter shows about 120 mA (≈1.3 W) leaving the pack in every lid-closed sleep, around 2.5% an hour, where a healthy M4 Air uses a small fraction of that. The draw is there with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off, after reboots, and on macOS 15 and macOS 27 alike, and it shows in logs back to mid-June. The cells are balanced and healthy, so the energy is going into the machine, not leaking from the battery. Whether that's a hardware fault or a sleep-state bug is still open.
